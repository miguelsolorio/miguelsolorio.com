Looking at the Data — ten interactive variations

The onboarding case study currently draws this figure as a static build-time SVG: two donuts, leader-line callouts, no JavaScript. Below are ten ways the same two numbers could behave once the reader can point at them — each built on a real charting library so the hover, focus and animation behaviour is the library's own, not an imitation.

Charts stack vertically by default. Every variation is themed, keyboard-reachable, and honours prefers-reduced-motion. Values are the published ones; they sum to 99% and 101%, so geometry runs on the normalised share while labels keep the authored number — same trade the shipping shortcode makes.

Notes for picking one

  • Colour. The shipped ramp fails two ordinal checks — its two palest teal steps are 0.054 apart in OKLCH lightness (0.06 is the floor where steps stop reading as separate) and the palest step is 1.17:1 against the page, invisible as a mark. Every ramp here is re-stepped on the same two hues and passes in both themes against the real card surfaces.
  • Libraries. Four are loaded here so each variation shows its library's true behaviour. Shipping any of them means one library, or re-implementing the winner as build-time SVG plus a small hover layer — the current shortcode ships zero JavaScript, which is worth keeping if the interaction earns little.
  • The 0% cases. "Recent folder" is absent from the new-user list and "New file" from the current-user list. Those shares are inside each segment's Other bucket, so the comparison charts draw them as a bounded ghost mark (≤ that segment's Other), never as a hard zero.
  • Naming. The source writes "Install Extensions" for new users and "Install Extension" for current ones. The comparison charts canonicalise to one label so the two segments line up.
